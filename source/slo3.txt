 proportions and differences in proportions. But other statistics
 require other distributions. In this stretch outcomes, I want to go

 and Student's t-distribution.

Probability distributions are mathematical functions. To define the
Chi^2 distribution, we actually need to define a couple other function

calculation, but may be quite new to students without that experience.

often called Euler's number.  It has this approximate

exponential function in mathematics.  Like all exponential functions,

The second definition is more unusual. Even students of calculus may

After the Chi^2 distribution, there is one more distribution that is
useful for the hypothesis test that we are doing this course. This one
also relies on the Gamma-function for its definition. It also has one
parameter, which is called degrees of freedom. 

The Student's t-distribution is a probability distribution defined by
the following expression. It relies on two parameters. The first is a
mean, \mu, which can be any real numbers. The second is a positive
whole number s, called the degrees of freedom.

Like the Chi^2 distribution, this is a very complicated mathematical
function definition and I do not expect the class to be able to work
with or calculate this function. Again, I want you to understand the
pieces that go into the function, and the whole idea of probability
distributions and area under curves. That said, I should say a few
things about this distribution. 

First, let me talk about the strange name. In what what is this a
student's distribution? This distribution was developed by a
statistician named William Gosset. He published articles in the early
20th century on statistics, but decided, for whatever reason, to
publish under the pseudonym Student. Therefore, this distribution that
he developed was simply know as Student's distribution. This
is strange and confusion, but we are now stuck with the name and it is
used widely. 

Next, unlike the Chi^2 distribution, Student's distribution has a
mean. In fact, it is remarkably similar to the normal distribution.
Look at graphs, it is actually pretty difficult, at a glance, to tell
the difference between Student's distribution and the normal
distribution. They both have very similar bell-shapes, with a peak at
the mean and symmetrical tails. 

That said, there are important differences. First, the normal
distribution's second parameter is the standard deviation. The
Student's distirbution's second parameter is this degrees of
freedom. Standard deviation for the Student's distribution can be
calculated, but it does not directly act at the paramger. 

Second, there are subtle difference in shape. The most notable is that
Student's distribution has a slightly wider shape, with slightly more
area in the tails. This is particularly true for low values of the
degrees of freedom parameter. As the degrees of freedom parameter
grows, Student's distribution becomes more an more like a normal
distribution with standard deviation 1. 

In the mathematical theory of probability, it was discovered (By
Gosset and other at the time) that the normal distribution was not
quite the correct model for the probability of statistics involving
means. Student's distribution was developed as an alternate that
corrects the small faults of the normal distribution. We will use it
for difference in means, single means, and ANOVA calculations. It
turns out to be a very useful and very commonly used distribution in
both probability and statistics.
